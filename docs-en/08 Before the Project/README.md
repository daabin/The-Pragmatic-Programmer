# Chapter 8 - Before the Project

<a id="d24e24301"></a>
At the very beginning of a project, you and the team need to learn the
requirements. Simply being told what to do or
listening to users is not enough: read Topic 45, [*The Requirements Pit*](<45 The Requirements Pit - requirements.md#requirements>) and learn how to avoid the common traps and pitfalls.

<a id="d24e24309"></a>
Conventional wisdom and constraint management are the topics of
Topic 46, [*Solving Impossible Puzzles*](<46 Solving Impossible Puzzles - outta_box.md#outta_box>). Whether you are performing
requirements, analysis, coding, or testing, difficult problems will crop
up. Most of the time, they won't be as difficult as they first appear
to be.

<a id="d24e24322"></a>
And when that impossible project comes up, we like to turn to our secret weapon: Topic 47, [*Working Together*](<47 Working Together - working_together.md#working_together>). And by “working together” we don't mean sharing a massive requirements document, flinging heavily cc'd emails or enduring endless meetings. We mean solving problems together while coding. We'll show you who you need and how to start.

<a id="d24e24335"></a>
Even though the Agile Manifesto begins with “Individuals and interactions over processes and tools,” virtually all “agile” projects begin with an ironic discussion of which process and which tools they'll use. But no matter how well thought out it is, and regardless of which “best practices” it includes, no method can replace thinking. You don't need any particular process or tool, what you do need is the Topic 48, [*The Essence of Agility*](<48 The Essence of Agility - essence_of_agility.md#essence_of_agility>).

<a id="d24e24352"></a>
With these critical issues sorted out before the project gets under
way, you can be better positioned to avoid “analysis paralysis” and
actually begin—and complete—your successful project.
